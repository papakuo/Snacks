Conquests of Northern Kings.

When Jabin, king of Hazor, heard of this, he sent to Jobab king of Madon, and to the king of Shimron, and to the king of Achshaph, and to the kings who were in the northern hill country, and in the Arabah south of Chinneroth, and in the lowland, and in Naphoth-dor on the west, to the Canaanites in the east and the west, the Amorites, the Hittites, the Perizzites, and the Jebusites in the hill country, and the Hivites under Hermon in the land of Mizpah. And they came out with all their troops, a great horde, in number like the sand that is on the seashore, with very many horses and chariots. And all these kings joined their forces and came and encamped together at the waters of Merom to fight against Israel. (Joshua 11: 1 to 5)

After seeing what God had done, the northern kings of Canaan were still determined to gather a great army to rebel against Israel. So, God reached out to help Israel again as described below.

And the Lord said to Joshua, “Do not be afraid of them, for tomorrow at this time I will give over all of them, slain, to Israel. You shall hamstring their horses and burn their chariots with fire.” So Joshua and all his warriors came suddenly against them by the waters of Merom and fell upon them. And the Lord gave them into the hand of Israel, who struck them and chased them as far as Great Sidon and Misrephoth-maim, and eastward as far as the Valley of Mizpeh. And they struck them until he left none remaining. And Joshua did to them just as the Lord said to him: he hamstrung their horses and burned their chariots with fire. (Joshua 11: 6 to 9)

Then Joshua continued the fighting as described below.

And all the cities of those kings, and all their kings, Joshua captured, and struck them with the edge of the sword, devoting them to destruction, just as Moses the servant of the Lord had commanded. But none of the cities that stood on mounds did Israel burn, except Hazor alone; that Joshua burned. And all the spoil of these cities and the livestock, the people of Israel took for their plunder. But every person they struck with the edge of the sword until they had destroyed them, and they did not leave any who breathed. Just as the Lord had commanded Moses his servant, so Moses commanded Joshua, and so Joshua did. He left nothing undone of all that the Lord had commanded Moses. (Joshua 11: 12 to 15)

The conquest continued for quite some time as described below.

Joshua made war a long time with all those kings. There was not a city that made peace with the people of Israel except the Hivites, the inhabitants of Gibeon. They took them all in battle. For it was the Lord's doing to harden their hearts that they should come against Israel in battle, in order that they should be devoted to destruction and should receive no mercy but be destroyed, just as the Lord commanded Moses. (Joshua 11: 18 to 20)

So, except Gibeonites, the entire Canaanites rebelled against Israel and were destroyed. Why were the Gibeonites or Hivites so different? They were part of Amorites, the descendants of Esau. Yet they kept tracking what had happened in the Exodus. So, when they encountered the Israelites, they could give a vivid description to convince the Israelites of their submission. Not only that, they prepared in detail even some time for the dry crumbly food and worn-out shoes and clothes. After being found out of their plot, they promised to be the cutters of wood and drawers of water for all the congregation. This is following the example of the Medians as described in Numbers 31. Moses went against Midian to execute the Lord's vengeance on Midian because they enticed Israel to yoke himself to Baal of Peor. Moses ordered to kill all of the Median men and women except all the young girls who had not known the man by lying with him to keep alive for Israelites. Those people became the servants of the congregation. So, when the Gibeonite offered themselves to be the servant of the congregation, they had no excuse to say no. Later on, those Gibeonites and Medians emerged to Nethinim to do physical labor to assist the Levites, such as carrying wood and drawing water. They even helped to rebuild the city wall of Jerusalem upon returning from exile as described in Ezra and Nehemiah. So, the covenant that the Gibeonite made with Israel was not just to survive but a commitment too. No wonder God not only didn't stop the covenant but honored it forever.

It seems that unlike the rest of Canaanites, the Gibeonite chose an unpopular way just as Jesus described below.

“Enter by the narrow gate. For the gate is wide and the way is easy that leads to destruction, and those who enter by it are many. For the gate is narrow and the way is hard that leads to life, and those who find it are few.” (Matthew 7: 13 14)

For us who are saved by grace, we are choosing a hard way to enter a narrow gate. It might seem to be unpopular, yet it's the only way to eternal life. The Gibeonite made it, with Jesus Christ as our savior, we could make it too. Immanuel.
